What IMC is

Two definitions were given. The lecturer's own is the one to reproduce; the textbook's is worth recognising.

Lecturer's definition
Integrated marketing communication is a concept under which companies carefully coordinate various communication channels in order to provide a clear, consistent, compelling and concise message about the company and its brand, and the result is measurable. In one line: no conflicting message, the same message across all platforms.
Textbook definition (AAAA)
A concept of marketing communications planning that recognizes the added value of a comprehensive plan that evaluates the strategic roles of a variety of communication disciplines (general advertising, direct marketing, sales promotion, public relations) and combines them to provide clarity, consistency and maximum communications impactThe qualitative value of an exposure through a given medium.BBC carries more impact than BTV for the same ad..

Why it matters for branding. Brand awareness is a driver of brand equityThe value a brand adds to a product beyond the product itself.Driven by awareness, recognition, recall, image and quality, and the customer relationship., and IMC is how awareness is built. The drivers of brand equity listed in class: value of the product, brand recognitionWhen consumers can identify a brand upon seeing or hearing it.Easier than recall: they need a cue., brand awareness, brand recallWhen consumers remember your brand on their own, unprompted.Harder than recognition., image and quality, and the quality of the customer relationship. Brand recall is when consumers remember your brand on their own. Brand recognition is when they can identify it on seeing or hearing it.

Why IMC grew

The lecturer gave five reasons, and all three notebooks have the same five. Learn these first.

  1. Change in consumer media behaviour
  2. Technological advancement in communication
  3. Allocation of budget among channels, and the use of multiple media channels
  4. Shift in the promotional budget
  5. Evolution of advertising agencies and their increasing accountability

One notebook carries a longer, ten-item list that contains the five above and adds: rise of digital and social media, fragmentation of mediaThe audience is spread thinly across many channels instead of gathered around a few mass media.One of the main reasons coordination became necessary., cost efficiency and ROI pressure, stronger brand identity and recall, improved customer experience, growth of data and marketing technology, cross-functional marketing teams, globalization of brands, and demand for storytelling and purpose. Useful if the question asks for "reasons" without a number.

The importance of IMC comes down to two things. Strategic integration of the communication functions avoids duplication and creates synergy among the promotional tools, so marketing is more efficient and more effective. And the environment is changing rapidly, in consumers, technology and media. The lecturer marked the second as the most important. The book's picture of that change is a list of shifts:

Evolution

IMC started during the 1980s. Before 1980 there was no such concept: only mass media (TV, radio, newspaper), few channels, and no need to coordinate them. After 1980 companies began using many communication channels, found the messages conflicting, and recognised that coordination was needed. Agencies emerged and were given that coordination job.

Trends and challenges

Emerging trends
Personal marketing · AI · very knowledgeable customers · fragmented mass media · influencer marketing · data-driven personalization · real-time and reactive marketing · video and immersive tech (AR, VR) · purpose and ethics · neuro-branding · agile and integrated · interactive content.
Branding challenges
Branding is the most challenging task now, because of: (a) reduction of the customer's attention span; (b) changes in the consumer decision journey; (c) a sharp increase in buying through online retail channels; (d) a shift in advertising and promotion expenditure to digital channels; (e) the rise of many-to-many communication, which gets out of the company's control.

The IMC Planning Process

Lectures 13 July, 13 August, 9, 10 and 17 September · Notes: all three

This is the syllabus. The lecturer taught from one flowchart, the model of the IMC planning process, and went down it as far as the Advertising box. Everything in this chapter is a step of that chart; Chapter 4 is the Advertising box opened up.

The model

Read it top to bottom. Four analysis steps set up the fifth, which is the actual programme: choose tools, then for each tool set objectives and strategy, then design the message and media. The last two boxes, implementing and monitoring, were not reached before the midterm. The dashed arrows on the left mean results feed back into every earlier step.

Step 1: Review of the marketing plan

The lecturer set this as a board question: what is a marketing plan, what does it contain, and why review it?

A marketing plan is the guideline for how a product will be marketed. It works at two levels, and both must be reviewed. The strategic level is STPSegmentation, targeting, positioning: the strategic core of the marketing plan.: segmentation, targeting, positioning. The tactical level is features, discounts, product differentiation.

Contents of a marketing plan
1 Executive summary · 2 Current market situation · 3 SWOT, PESTLE, or market opportunity and issue analysis · 4 Marketing objectives · 5 Marketing strategy (STP) · 6 Action plan · 7 Financial projection · 8 Implementation · 9 Control and feedback.
Why review it
The promotional plan must be aligned with the marketing plan. If it is not, it will not be successful and will not be accepted. Write the answer logically: the plan tells you the target, the positioning and the objectives that the promotion has to serve.

Step 2: Analysis of the promotional programme situation

Two halves: internal and external. The external half is mostly consumer behaviour, which is why the lecturer asked why it is necessary to review consumer buyer behaviour in IMC.

Internal analysis
Promotional department organization · the firm's ability to implement the promotional programme · agency evaluation and selection · review of previous programme results.
External analysis
Consumer behaviour analysis · market segmentationDividing the market into groups of customers with shared characteristics.Bases: demographic, psychographic, behavioural, geographic. Needed because all customers are not the same. and target marketing · market positioning.

Consumer behaviour

Every step has a role for IMC. Need recognitionThe moment a consumer notices a gap between where they are and where they want to be.Triggered by running out, dissatisfaction, new wants, related purchases, marketing, or new products. is triggered by being out of stock, dissatisfaction (expectation greater than performance), new needs or wants, related product purchases, market-induced recognition, or new products. Factors influencing consumer behaviour: cultural (culture, sub-culture, social class) · social (reference groups, family, roles and status) · personal (occupation, economic situation, personality, lifestyle, age and life-cycle) · psychological (motivation, perception, learning, attitude).

The survey questions that turn this into IMC: who buys the product, how the decision is made, what the customer actually buys, why, where, when.

Segmentation and targeting

Market segmentation divides the market into groups based on shared characteristics. The bases are demographic, psychographic, behavioural and geographic. It is essential because all customers are not the same. Target market approaches: mass or undifferentiated · segment or differentiated · niche · micromarketing (local or individual).

Step 3: Analysis of the communication process

The sender is the advertiser, the company itself. The receiver is the audience, the respondent, the customer.

The message (its underlying content) · the channel, source or spokesperson · the response · the feedback. All components must align through the IMC process.

The lecturer set two questions here: what is a celebrity, and how are celebrities selected. A celebrity is a person widely recognized by the public for their personal achievement. Celebrity credibility
To the audience: ethical, trustworthy, knowledgeable, skillful, experienced, believable, honest, unbiased.
Celebrity attractiveness
Likeability (affection from physical appearance), familiarity, similarity.
Celebrity / brand matching
A local brand takes a local celebrity, a global brand a global one. The celebrity's personality must match the brand personality.
Celebrity / audience matching
A young target market takes a young celebrity; a target aged 60 takes someone older.
Budget and conflict of interest
The cost, and whether the celebrity has already signed with a competitor.

Step 4: Budget determination

Affordable method
The company sets the budget at the level it can afford. Small enterprises.
Percentage-of-sales method
A fixed percentage, say 3 or 5 percent, of current or forecast sales. Medium enterprises.
Competitive parity method
The budget matches competitors' spending, using overall industry sales data.
Objective and task method
(a) Determine the promotion objectives, such as awareness or market share. (b) Determine the tasks needed to achieve them. (c) Estimate the cost of performing the tasks.

The IMC planner has to know which method the company follows and work within it. Advertising

Lectures 19 and 20 August, 2, 3, 23 and 30 September · Notes: all three

The one tool the lecturer worked through in full, using the five-question template: definition, uniqueness, objectives, major decisions, evaluation. This is the heaviest chapter in every notebook and the one to know best.

What advertising is

Advertising is any paid form of non-personal presentation and promotion of goods, services or ideas by an identified sponsor. It is the largest tool of promotion. Three tests: it is paid (the sponsor buys time or space), it is non-personal, and the sponsor is identified. It can be video, printed or audio. What is advertised: goods, services, ideas, persons, places, and the rest of the ten entities of marketing.

Platforms
TV, newspaper, radio, magazine, journal, billboard, poster, leaflet, packaging, website, social media, a wall, a jersey, point-of-purchase displays, vehicles.
Uniqueness
Impersonal, non-personal: the audience is not obliged to pay attention · public presentation · amplified expressivenessAdvertising can dramatise the company and product through artful use of print, sound and colour. · pervasivenessThe message is repeated many times.One of advertising's distinctive qualities., the message is repeated many times.
Functions
Informing (the basic, first function) · persuading (the second: build selective demandDemand for a particular brand, such as Nestlé coffee.Most advertising builds selective demand. for the brand, encourage switching, show how the product differs) · reminding (Coca-Cola keeps running new ads so customers do not forget) · adding value (changing perception) · assisting the other promotion tools and other company efforts, to establish brand credibility.
Objectives
To inform: a new product, how to use it properly, how it works, alternatives. To remind: keep top of mind, off-season reminders. To persuade: comparative and encouraging messages. To add value: change perception. To assist others: regular messages.

How advertising works

Each ad nudges the customer one rung: from not knowing the brand, to knowing it, to expecting something of it, to trying it, to forming beliefs, to an attitude, to a reinforced attitude, to loyalty. Beliefs are reinforced by repeated experience; attitude is reinforced by repeated messages. One notebook labels the ladder the AIDA model.

Be ready to explain the difference.

What makes advertising effective, and creative

From the slides, the desirable qualities of an advertisement: it should never promise more than it can deliver · it must be informative · the message must be clear, not complex · it must align with the overall marketing strategy · it must highlight the product's value and the consumer's benefit · it must be creative · it must find a unique way to break through and gain attention · it must be persuasive.

CreativityUniqueness, and the ability to grab the customer's attention and generate a response. in advertising is uniqueness, and the ability to grab the customer's attention and generate a response. Creating the message means keeping it short and simple, easy to understand, creative and consistent.

Message decision: appeal and execution

Message strategy has two parts. Creating the message: focus on customer benefits; the message statement tends to be plain, straightforward and a positioning point. Selecting the appeal: the appeal is the underlying content of the message, and there are three.

Emotional appeal
A psychological attack: affection, prestige, safety and security, fear, love, comfort, ambition, happiness, joy, nostalgia, sentiment, excitement, arousal, stimulation, sorrow, grief, pride, achievement, self-esteem, embarrassment, pleasure.
Moral appeal
The environment, CSR, well-being, ethical issues.

Media decision

The lecturer called this the most important of the five decisions. It runs in four steps: how many people and how often, which media types, which media vehiclesA specific outlet within a medium: the medium is TV, the vehicle is Jamuna TV., and when.

Step 1: reach, frequency, impact
Reach is the percentage of the target exposed to the ad. Frequency is how many times they are exposed. Impact is the qualitative value of an exposure through a given medium: BBC has more impact than BTV.
Step 2: major media types
TV, newspaper, magazine, radio, journal, billboard, outdoor, socials, web. Chosen on the media habits of the target, the nature of the product, the type of message, and cost.
Step 3: specific media vehicles
The medium is TV; the vehicle is Jamuna TV. Balance media cost against media factors: audience quality and attention.
Step 4: media timing
Scheduling the ads over the course of a year. The patterns are continuity, flightingAdvertising in bursts, with periods of no advertising in between. and pulsingA low level of continuous advertising plus heavier bursts.Combines continuity and flighting..

Sales promotion

Sales promotion is a short-term incentive to encourage immediate purchase or sale of a product or service: a mass-communication technique offering a short-term incentive. Its forms: discount, extra quantity, coupon, cashback, free samples, rebate, refund, allowance, commission, buy one get one free, membership.

Uniqueness
It invites immediate purchase · it draws attention · it is measurable, the sales effect can be counted · it gives an incentive or reward (a free tiffin box, a toothbrush) that must integrate with the product.
Objectives
Stock clearance · trial, to attract new customers · repeat purchase (membership) · increase consumption and sales · increase market share · enhance the IMC process · get the trade to stock or carry a new product.
Why it grew
Growing power of retailers · declining brand loyalty · increased promotional sensitivity · brand proliferationToo many brands competing in the same category.One reason sales promotion grew. · fragmentation of consumer markets · short-term focus of marketers · increased accountability · competition · clutter · technological advancement.
AdvantagesDisadvantages
Increased salesBrand image damaged; brand loyalty decreases
Influences consumer behaviourConsumers become more price-sensitive
Price discrimination, by segmentShort-term orientation
Repeat purchaseConflict of interest with other sales promotion tools

Major decisions, in order: establish the sales promotion objective; select the tools; develop and implement the programme; determine its length; evaluate it. The slides open the third step into five decisions: decide the size of the incentive, set conditions for participation, decide how to promote and distribute the programme, determine its length, and evaluate it by sales effect, repeat purchase, loyalty and market performance.

Recall in one line each
  • Sales promotion = short-term incentive for immediate purchase. Unique: immediate, attention, measurable, reward.
  • Consumer-orientedSales promotion aimed at the final buyer to pull the product through the channel.Samples, coupons, premiums, contests, rebates, bonus packs. tools pull (samples, coupons, premiums, contests, rebates, bonus packs, price-offs, frequency programs, events). Trade-orientedSales promotion aimed at retailers and distributors to push the product through the channel.Allowances, POP displays, training, trade shows, cooperative advertising. tools push (incentives, allowances, POP, training, trade shows, cooperative advertising).
  • Decisions: objective → tools → develop and implement (size of incentive, conditions, distribution, length, evaluation) → length → evaluate.
