Social media marketing

The first tool after the midterm: promotion on the platforms where the audience already is

The midterm stopped at advertising, and class test 2 covered sales promotion, personal selling and public relations. This test picks up the digital tools that sit beside them in the promotional mixThe combination of all the promotional tools a company uses.Advertising, sales promotion, PR, personal selling, direct marketing and the digital tools are all parts of one mix.. Every one is taught to the same template the professor used for advertising: what the tool is, what makes it unique, what it is for, which platforms or tools it uses, what decisions you take, and how you evaluate it. An answer that walks those six steps will always have a shape.

The brief calls this topic social marketingMarketing used to advance a social cause or change behaviour for social good.Not the same as social media marketing. The class used the second meaning.. In the notes and in the lecture it is social media marketing: using social mediaUsing social media platforms to do promotion activities.The platform is only the channel; what makes it a separate tool is that the audience can answer, share or ignore in the same feed. platforms to do promotion activities. Social marketing in the textbook sense, marketing a social cause, is a different idea and is not what the class covered.

What it is

Social media marketing is the use of social media platforms to carry out promotion activities. The definition is deliberately wide, because the platforms carry almost every other tool: an advertisement, a sales promotion, a public relations announcement and a personal-selling conversation can all happen inside the same feed. What makes it a separate tool is not the message but the channel, and the channel changes the rules. The audience is not sitting still to be spoken to. It is scrolling, and it can answer, share or ignore.

Social media marketing
Using social media platforms to do promotion activities. It is a distribution channel for the other tools as much as a tool in its own right, which is why the notes treat its reachThe number of different people exposed to a message.Reach is size of audience; frequency is how often each person sees it. A large reach with low engagement is expensive noise. as earned rather than bought.
Social marketing
Marketing used to advance a social cause or change behaviour for social good. A different term from social media marketing; the class did not cover it under this heading.
Platform
The site or app that carries the content: Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn, YouTube, Snapchat, X, Pinterest, TikTok.

The platforms, and what each is good at

The notes name five platforms in the overview, work through six in detail, and name Snapchat without writing it up. Each has an audience and a native format, and the platform choice follows from those two facts, not from which one is fashionable.

PlatformWhat it is used forThe limit to remember
FacebookThe mass platform. Many-to-many communication, wide reach, organic reach, well-targeted paid options, budget friendly, quick to generate likesLack of trust in the feed, ads feel intrusive and annoying, and there is no guarantee that people will click
InstagramYoung generation targeting, visual credibility, strong for lifestyle and fashionCrowded and image-led, so a weak visual gets no attention; the page left the cons column blank
X (Twitter)Fast public conversation, and customer service in the openFlooded with tweets, which makes a single message hard to see and easy to miss
PinterestFemale targeting, planning and inspiration, long life for a pinVisual only, so a product that needs explanation does not fit
LinkedInProfessional people, achievement-oriented content, job seeking, B2BBusiness to business: selling to another organisation rather than to a consumer.LinkedIn and case studies are the natural B2B tools.Formal tone and a narrower audience; overt selling is poorly received

The page is not a ranking. Facebook is the broadest and the cheapest to test, Instagram is the youngest, LinkedIn is the most professional, Pinterest is the most female and the most visual, and YouTube is the only one built for long video. Choosing between them is the second major decision below.

Paid, owned and earned

Social media activity is grouped by who controls the space. The three groups behave differently, cost differently and are believed differently, so a programme that uses only one of them is unbalanced.

Paid media
Space bought on a platform: boosted posts, feed ads, sponsored stories, influencer fees. You control the message and the targeting, and you pay for every impression.
Owned media
Space the brand controls: its page, profile, channel and website. Nobody can take it away and no media cost is paid, but reaching an audience still depends on the platform's algorithm.
Earned media
Coverage and conversation the brand did not buy: shares, comments, reviews, tags, reposts, press pick-up. It carries the most credibility because a customer chose to pass it on, and the least control.
Organic reach
The number of people who see a post without paid promotion. It is falling on most platforms, which is why owned media alone is rarely enough.

Uniqueness

Six qualities are listed. They are the reason a brand cannot simply reuse a television commercial on a phone screen and expect the same result.

Six qualities
1ShareabilityThe audience can pass the message on in one tap, so distribution is partly done by the customer.
2OptimizationEvery post can be tested, compared and improved, and the platform reports the result while the campaign is still running.
3ViralityA message can travel far beyond the paid audience, at no extra media cost, if it earns the share.
4ReviewThe audience talks back in public: comments, ratings, complaints. The brand is reviewed whether it asked or not.
5EditabilityA post can be changed or deleted after it goes live, which no newspaper or television spot allows.
6TargetThe platform knows enough about the user to put the message in front of a narrow group rather than a mass audience.
The trade behind the six

Shareability, virality and review are the same property seen from three sides: the audience has control. That is what makes social media cheap and credible, and also what makes it impossible to guarantee. Paid media buys reach; earned media has to be deserved.

Advantages and disadvantages

AdvantagesDisadvantages
Many-to-many communication: the brand speaks to the audience and the audience speaks to each otherLack of trust: users know the space is commercial and discount the claim
Reach, including organic reach that costs nothing in mediaAds are intrusive and annoying, and the platform is built to scroll past them
Well-targeted: age, place, interest and behaviour can all narrow the audienceNo guarantee that people will click, however good the targeting
Cost-based results and analytics to track them, so spend can be judged against responseThe same analytics are controlled by the platform, which reports its own numbers
Budget friendly, and a quick way to generate likes and a first audienceLikes are easy to buy and weak as a measure of anything; engagement can be vanity

Major decisions

The notes give two decisions on the platform page and finish the programme on the next page, so the full sequence is four steps: objective, platform, programme, evaluation.

Objective

The object of social media marketing is one or more of four communication jobs. It has to be chosen before the platform, because the platform is chosen to fit the job.

AwarenessMake the brand known to people who do not know it yet.
PersuadeMove someone who knows the brand towards preferring it.
MotivateGive a reason to act now: enter, buy, visit, share.
InformTell the audience something it needs to know: a launch, a change, a correction.
Platform selection
Choose the platform that fits the objective and aligns with the company's image. A professional brand on Snapchat and a youth brand on LinkedIn are both sending a message by the choice itself.
Executable programme
Turn the decision into a plan someone can run: who posts, what is posted, how often, which format, which budget, which paid support.
Evaluation
Read the platform's own numbers: likes, views, comments and analytics. The notes name Facebook's four because they are the ones every page reports.

How it contributes to IMC

The exam question is a sentence on the page: how does social media marketing contribute to IMCIntegrated marketing communication: coordinating every channel so the audience receives one clear, consistent message, and measuring the result.The internet contributes to it by carrying the same message, hearing the answer, and reporting a measurable response.? The answer has three parts, and the third is the one most answers miss.

First, it carries the same message to the audience the other tools are already addressing, in the place where that audience spends its time. Second, it makes the message two-way, so the brand hears the response instead of guessing it. Third, it hands back a measurement: likes, views, comments and analytics tell the planner which message and which platform worked, and that evidence is fed into the next round of the programme. IMC is a loop, not a line, and social media is the part of the loop that reports back fastest.

Influencer marketing

The notebook lists the tool but does not open it; this part is built from Belch and the standard model

The notebook names influencer marketingA person with an audience is partnered with to promote a brand to that audience.The brand buys credibility that already exists, rather than building it from scratch. twice, once in the promotion mix and once in the tools list, and never gives it a page. The class test does name it, so it needs a section. Everything below is built from the two ideas Belch does develop at length, source credibilityThe audience's judgement that a source has expertise and can be trusted.Belch treats expertise and trustworthiness as the two parts. An influencer who lacks either is just a paid announcer. and word of mouthInformation about a product passed from one consumer to another.It is more believed than advertising because the sender has nothing obvious to gain., and from the model the industry actually uses. It is marked as such so you know what came from the lecture and what did not.

What it is

Influencer marketing is a form of social media marketing in which a person who already has an audience is paid, or otherwise partnered with, to recommend or show a brand to that audience. The brand is not buying the platform's space. It is buying the trust that sits between one person and their followers.

Belch explains why that trust is worth buying. Personal sources are often the most influential factor in a purchase, and word-of-mouth information passed from one individual to another is perceived as more reliable than the same claim made through a formal channel such as advertising. A source is believed when it has both expertise, it knows the product, and trustworthiness, it has no obvious reason to lie. An influencer is a source with both, rented by the brand.

Influencer marketing
A person with an audience is partnered with to promote a brand to that audience. The asset being bought is credibility, not media space.
Influencer
A person who has built an audience on a platform and can move that audience's opinion or behaviour.
Source credibility
The audience's judgement that a source has expertise and can be trusted. Belch treats these as the two components that decide whether a message is believed.
Word of mouth
Information about a product passed from one consumer to another. It is more believed than advertising because the sender has nothing obvious to gain.
Opinion leader
A person who is influential within a group because others look to them for advice. The older idea behind the modern influencer.
Disclosure
Telling the audience that the recommendation is paid for. Without it the endorsement borrows trust it did not earn.

Why it works

Four reasons, and they are the marks in a "why is influencer marketing effective" question.

Four reasons
1CredibilityThe audience already trusts the person, so the claim arrives with belief attached. Belch's point that personal sources are more influential than formal ones.
2TargetingThe influencer's audience is already a segment: a hobby, a profession, a life stage. The brand reaches the segment without building it.
3AttentionA familiar face stops the scroll in a way a brand page cannot. The creator's format is native to the platform.
4ContentThe brand gets a piece of content made in the creator's voice, which usually performs better than a brand-made post and can be reused as paid creative.

The tiers

Influencers are sorted by audience size, and the sort matters because reach and trust move in opposite directions. The follower bands below are the industry convention, not a law; different agencies draw the lines in different places.

TierAudienceWhat it buysWhat it costs
Nano1,000 to 10,000The highest trust and engagement, a niche audience, cheap to testVery small reach, and many creators to manage
Micro10,000 to 100,000A defined niche with a real relationship, good engagement, affordableLimited reach, and the creator may be new to brand work
Macro100,000 to 1,000,000Reach plus production quality, a recognised face in a categoryHigher fee, lower engagement rate, less intimate trust
Mega or celebrity1,000,000 and aboveMass awareness and status, fastVery high fee, weakest engagement, and the audience knows it is an ad

The practical rule that follows: use nano and micro creators for trust and conversionThe moment an audience member takes the action the campaign asked for.A sale, a sign-up, a download or a booking. Reach is how many saw it; conversion is how many acted., macro for reach within a category, and mega for awareness only. A campaign that uses one mega creator and nothing else has bought reach and rented very little trust.

Choosing the right influencer

Follower count is the easiest number to read and the worst one to decide on. The notes' platform logic applies here too: the choice has to align with the company's image, and the audience has to be the company's audience.

Selection criteria
1RelevanceThe creator's subject and audience match the product. A fitness creator for a snack brand, not a celebrity for anything.
2Engagement rateLikes, comments and shares as a share of followers. It shows whether the audience is listening; a high count with low engagement is a bought audience.
3Audience fitAge, location, language and buying power of the followers, checked from the platform's audience report, not assumed from the creator's own image.
4Credibility and historyWhat the creator has promoted before, and whether the audience still believes the recommendations.
5Brand safetyThe creator's other content, politics, language and controversies. A scandal transfers to the brand in hours.
6Content qualityWhether the creator can make the brand look good in their own format, without losing the voice that made them worth hiring.
7Cost and fitFee against expected reach and conversion. The largest fee is not the largest return.

Advantages and disadvantages

AdvantagesDisadvantages
Credibility borrowed from a trusted source, which advertising cannot buy directlyTrust is rented, not owned: the audience may follow the creator away, or stop believing them
Precise targeting through the creator's niche audienceAudience data is reported by the creator or the platform and is hard to verify
Native content that fits the platform and is not skipped like an adDisclosureTelling the audience that a recommendation is paid for.Without it the endorsement borrows trust it did not earn, and the audience eventually finds out. is required, and a visible paid partnership weakens the endorsement
Lower production cost than a campaign film, and fast to launchFees have risen, and a celebrity fee can buy less attention than a hundred micro creators
Content can be reused as paid advertising and across channelsOne mistake by the creator becomes the brand's crisis
Works at every tier, so it can be tested small before it is scaledMeasurement is difficult: the sale often happens off the platform, days later

Major decisions

Objective
Awareness, persuasion, trial or sales. The objective decides the tier: awareness tolerates a mega creator, conversion does not.
Type and selection
Celebrity, expert, micro creator or existing customer, chosen against the criteria above.
Brief and contract
What the creator must show, what they may change, when it runs, what rights the brand has to reuse it, and the disclosure line. Too tight a brief loses the voice; too loose a brief loses the message.
Programme
How many creators, over what period, on which platforms, with what paid support behind the posts.
Evaluation
Reach, engagement, clicks, code or link redemptions, sales lift and sentiment, read against the objective rather than against follower count.
The ethics line

A paid recommendation presented as an ordinary opinion is not clever marketing, it is a misrepresentation. Disclosure protects the audience and, in the long run, protects the creator's trust, which is the asset the brand is paying for. Belch makes the same point about stealth marketing: once the audience discovers the trick, the word of mouth turns negative.

Content marketing

Making something worth reading, watching or listening to, and letting it do the selling

The notebook gives content marketingCreating video, podcast, article, infographic and similar material about the company and its brand, to earn attention and trust.The audience chooses to consume it, which is why it is believed more readily than an ad. one definition and one comparison, and the comparison is the part examiners like. The definition is the creation of video, podcast, article, infographic and similar material about the company and its brand. The comparison is between traditional marketing and content marketing.

What it is

Content marketing is the creation and distribution of material that is useful, interesting or entertaining to a defined audience, about the company and its brand, in order to earn attention and trust rather than to interrupt for a sale. The company is still selling. It is doing it by being worth the audience's time first.

Two words in the definition do most of the work. About the company and its brand keeps it marketing and not publishing: a recipe is content, but a recipe that shows how the company's product is used is content marketing. Creation makes it a production job, not a media-buying job: the brand owns the asset and can use it again.

Content marketing
The creation of video, podcast, article, infographic and similar material about the company and its brand, made to earn attention and trust rather than to interrupt.
Content
The asset itself: the article, video, episode, image or guide. One piece of content can be cut into many posts for many platforms.
Evergreen content
Content that stays useful for months or years, so it keeps earning attention long after it was published. A how-to guide is evergreen; a launch announcement is not.
Inbound marketing
Pulling the audience in with something it chose to look at, instead of pushing a message at it. Content marketing is the main inbound tool.
Blog marketing
Content marketing on a blog: long-form posts that give detailed information about the company and its brand, and build trust and credibility over time.

Traditional marketing against content marketing

The notes ask for the difference directly. The cleanest way to answer is not a list of definitions but a set of paired contrasts, because each contrast is one mark.

Traditional marketingContent marketing
Interrupts: the ad breaks into what the audience was doingAttracts: the audience chooses to read, watch or listen
Paid media is the main cost, and the message stops when the budget stopsProduction is the main cost, and the asset keeps working after the budget stops
Pushes a claim at a mass audiencePulls a defined audience with something useful
A campaign with a start and an endAn ongoing programme that accumulates
Says how good the product isShows what the product does, or answers the question around it
Measured by reach and immediate responseMeasured by attention, trust, leads and retention over time
One-way: the audience receivesTwo-way: the audience comments, shares and asks
Works while it runsCompounds: old content keeps being found
Do not overstate it

Content marketing does not replace advertising. Advertising buys reach now; content earns trust over time. The two are strongest together, with the content giving the advertising something to point at and the advertising giving the content an audience. The exam answer that says one is dead has taken a side the course did not take.

The forms

The notes name four forms and then say "and so on", so the list is open. What matters is that the form follows the audience and the platform: a podcast suits a commute, an infographic suits a statistic, a video suits a demonstration, and a blog post suits a search query.

The forms

The four named in the notes, then the forms that belong to the same family.

VideoDemonstration, explainer, interview, short-form clip. The most expensive to make and the most native to every platform.
PodcastLong-form audio, usually a conversation. Cheap to produce, intimate, and easy to consume while doing something else.
ArticleA written piece published on the brand's site or a partner's. The workhorse of search-driven content.
InfographicA visual that turns a set of numbers or steps into one picture. Highly shareable, and it earns links.
Blog postThe long-form article on the company's own site. Detailed information about the company and its brand, and the page the search engine finds.
NewsletterA regular email to an audience that opted in. The brand owns the list, which no platform can take away.
Case studyA customer's problem and result, told at length. The strongest form for B2B and for high-consideration purchases.
Guide or ebookA longer reference document, usually gated behind a form so it also captures a lead.
Social postThe short form of any of the above, cut for the platform's format.
WebinarA live or recorded session where the audience learns something and the brand demonstrates expertise.

Blog marketing

The notebook treats blog marketingContent marketing on a blog: long-form posts that give detailed information about the company and its brand.Its uniqueness is trust and credibility, because a post that answers a question without selling is believed. as its own heading, with two points: detailed information regarding the company and its brand, and uniqueness in trust and credibility. It is the oldest content form and still the one that search engines reward, because a blog post is a page that can answer a specific question.

A blog post earns trust by being more useful than the sales page. The reader arrives with a question, gets an answer that does not immediately demand a purchase, and leaves with the company's name attached to the answer. That is why the notes put trust and credibility under uniqueness: the post is not asking for anything, so it is believed. The cost is time. A blog is the slowest tool in the mix, and it only pays after enough posts exist to be found.

Uniqueness

Why content is different
1Pull, not pushThe audience chooses to consume it, so attention is volunteered rather than bought.
2Trust and credibilityA piece that helps without selling is believed, which is the blog point applied to every form.
3Long lifeA campaign ends; a good article or video keeps being found. The cost is paid once and the return continues.
4Search valueA page that answers a query can be found by someone who was not in the audience yet, which no paid post can do.
5ShareabilityUseful content is passed on, so distribution is partly earned.
6MeasurableTraffic, time on page, downloads, leads and sales can all be traced, though often over a longer window than an ad.
7OwnedThe asset sits on the brand's site or feed, so a platform change cannot delete it.

Major decisions

Objective
Awareness, trust, leads, retention or search visibility. Content built for every objective at once is built for none.
Audience and question
Who is being served, and what are they already searching for or asking? The content answers that question, not the brand's favourite claim.
Format
Article, video, podcast, infographic or a mix. Chosen for where the audience is and what the idea needs.
Distribution
Owned site, newsletter, social, search, partner or paid support. Content that is not distributed is a cost, not an asset.
Calendar and budget
How often, by whom, and at what production cost. Consistency matters more than volume.
Evaluation
Traffic, time on page, engagement, downloads, leads, conversion and retention, read over the period the objective needs.
The trap in evaluation

Content marketing is usually judged on a shorter clock than it works on. A video that gets few views in week one may still be the best sales asset in year two. Say what the objective was and measure against that, not against the first week's reach.

Attention marketing

The attention economy, and the shortest form of video

Attention marketingPromotion designed to draw or grab attention in a crowded feed, usually within the first seconds.It is not a channel but a way of treating every channel, and it is the direct answer to advertising clutter. starts from one fact: there is more content trying to be seen than there is attention to see it. Every other tool in the mix assumes it can get a hearing. This one is about getting the hearing at all.

What it is

Attention marketing is promotion built to draw or grab the attention of an audience in a crowded feed, usually in the first seconds, before the audience decides to scroll past. The notes put it beside the attention economy: the idea that attention is a scarce resource, and that platforms, publishers and brands are all competing to capture a fixed amount of it.

It is not a separate channel like social media or the internet. It is a way of treating every channel, and it is closest to advertising's oldest problem. Belch puts the same problem plainly: consumers channel-surf past most commercials, and the sheer number of messages makes it difficult for any one of them to break through the clutterThe volume of competing messages an audience meets.Clutter is the condition attention marketing exists to beat, and the reason a slow opening loses.. Attention marketing is the deliberate answer to that.

Attention marketing
Promotion designed to draw or grab attention in a crowded feed, usually within the first seconds of exposure.
Attention economy
The market in which attention is the scarce resource and content competes for a limited supply of it.
Scarcity
There is more content available than there is human attention to spend on it, so attention has a price.
Scroll-stop
The moment a piece of content interrupts the automatic scroll and earns a look. The first job of short-form attention marketing.
Hook
The opening line, frame or sound that decides whether the viewer stays. In short video it is the first one to three seconds.
Clutter
The volume of competing messages an audience meets. Clutter is the condition attention marketing exists to beat.

The attention economy

Three forces make attention scarce, and all three are recent.

Three forces
1Abundance of contentAnyone with a phone can publish. Supply rose from a few channels to an unlimited feed, while the audience's hours stayed the same.
2Algorithmic distributionThe platform decides what each user sees, ranked by predicted engagement. Content that does not hold attention is not shown, however good it is.
3Shorter attention spanThe audience has learned to judge a piece in a second or two, and to leave without guilt. The notes on the branding challenges list the reduction of the customer's attention span as one of the forces reshaping IMC.

The consequence is a shift in what is being bought. Traditional media buys exposure: a spot in front of a programme. Attention marketing buys the moment of contact and then has to earn the next second. That is why the measurement moves from reach to watch time.

The platforms and the format

The notes name TikTok and reels, and give the uniqueness in four words: the shortest form of videos. Short vertical video is the native unit of the attention economy because it is built to be judged instantly and swiped instantly.

PlatformThe formatWhat decides the result
TikTokFull-screen vertical video, sound on, the For You feedThe first frame and the first second; the algorithm then tests it on a small audience
Instagram ReelsShort vertical video inside Instagram, sound onThe hookThe opening line, frame or sound that decides whether the viewer stays.In short video it is the first one to three seconds, and it is the one variable worth testing. and the share; Reels reach people who do not follow the account
YouTube ShortsShort vertical video inside YouTubeRetention and repeat views; a short can feed the long-form channel
StoriesVertical, full-screen, gone in a dayImmediacy and a direct link; lower production value is acceptable

The form has rules of its own. It is watched with the sound on but the captions read, it is vertical, it is held in one hand, and it can be abandoned at any frame. A television commercial dropped into this format fails on every one of those points, which is why the notes treat short video as a distinct tool rather than a cut-down ad.

Uniqueness

Why short video is different
1Shortest form of videoThe whole message is delivered in seconds, so there is no time to build up to it. The point has to be in the opening.
2Instant judgementThe viewer decides in about a second. Production value cannot save a slow first frame.
3Native to the feedIt looks like the content around it, so it is not skipped the way an obvious ad is.
4Sound and motionMusic, voice and movement carry emotion faster than text, which is why the format travels.
5Algorithmic reachA good short can be shown to people who do not follow the brand, so distribution is earned by retention rather than bought.
6Cheap to testMany variations can be made and compared at low cost, which suits a format where the hook is the variable.

How attention is actually won

Attention is won in the first seconds and lost in the next ones. The structure below is the one short-form creators use, and it maps onto the classic sequence: attention first, interest second, action last.

The hook, then the hold
1Pattern interruptSomething unexpected in the first frame: a strange image, a direct question, a number that does not fit.
2Curiosity gapSay enough to open a question and not enough to answer it. The viewer stays to close the gap.
3Relevance in the first wordName the audience or the problem immediately, so the right viewer knows it is for them.
4EmotionSurprise, humour, fear, recognition or delight. Emotion travels faster than information.
5Motion and soundMovement, a cut, a sound cue. A static frame loses to a moving one.
6CaptionsMost feeds are watched with sound off at first, so the words have to be on screen.
7Length that earns itselfEvery extra second has to be paid for by the last one. Cut until removing anything would lose the point.
Attention is not persuasion

A shock hook can win the view and lose the brand. If the content promises something the product does not deliver, the attention converts into resentment, and the platform learns to show it to fewer people. The honest use of the tool is to make the real value impossible to scroll past, not to disguise a weak offer.

Major decisions

Objective
Awareness, a visit, a follow or a sale. A hook built for awareness is not the same hook that sells.
Audience and platform
Who is being stopped, and where are they already scrolling? TikTok, Reels and Shorts have different audiences and different lengths.
The hook
The first frame, the first line and the first sound. This is the decision that decides everything after it.
Format and length
Vertical, captioned, sound-led, and as short as the idea allows. The platform's own length limit is not a target.
Testing
Make several hooks for the same body and let the platform's numbers choose. The variable is the opening, not the whole film.
Evaluation
Three-second views, watch time, average view duration, completion rate, shares, saves, follows and click-throughThe share of people who click a link or an ad after seeing it.It measures whether attention turned into a visit, not whether the visit turned into a sale., read against the objective. The interactive medium, its vocabulary, and what it adds to IMC

The notebook introduces this topic as internet or interactive marketing, and the two names are the point. The internet is not just another place to put an ad. It is the first medium in the mix that answers back, and that changes what marketing can do and how it is measured.

The exam shorthand

E-business is the whole company online. E-commerce is the transaction. E-marketing is the persuasion. E-selling is the sale. E-trading is the swap. If you can place the five on one line from widest to narrowest, you can answer the question in one sentence.

Internet, intranet and extranet

The three networks are distinguished by who is allowed in, not by the technology. All three run on the same protocols; the difference is the boundary of access.

Internet
The connection of all computers all over the world. Open to anyone with a connection.
Intranet
The connection of all computers within one organization. Private, and used by employees for internal information and work.
Extranet
The connection of computers between a particular organization and its selected outside partners: suppliers, dealers, distributors. Private, but shared beyond the walls.

Uniqueness

The notes give five qualities, and Belch adds a sixth that belongs with them. Read them as a set: the first three make the medium cheap and wide, the last three make it personal and measurable.

Six qualities
1Global reachA page or a campaign is available everywhere at once, with no distribution network to build.
2Cost effectiveA small brand can publish to the same network as a large one. The cost is in production and media, not in access.
324/7 availabilityThe shop, the catalogue and the message never close, so the customer can act at the hour that suits them.

How internet marketing contributes to IMC

The written question on the page is exactly this: how does internet marketing contribute to the IMC? Five contributions, and they are the marks.

Five contributions
1One message, everywhereThe same claim can be carried on the site, the ad, the email and the social page, which is what IMC asks for: clarity, consistency and no conflicting message.
2Two-way contactThe audience answers, so the planner learns what the market thinks instead of inferring it from sales.
3Measurement of the whole programmeClicks, views and purchases give the IMC plan a feedback loop. The other tools can be judged against the digital response.

Measuring the effectiveness of promotion

Every tool is judged on two levels. The communication effect is what happened inside the customer: awareness, knowledge, attitude, preference. The sales effect is what happened at the till. Advertising and public relations are usually read on the first, sales promotion and personal selling on the second, and IMC reads both because the programme is meant to do both.

The methods are the familiar ones: pre-testing before the campaign, post-testing after it, recall and recognition tests for communication, and sales, market share and redemption counts for behaviour. The trap is attribution. Everything runs at once, so a sales rise is not proof that the campaign caused it. The internet helps here, because a code, a link or a landing page can tie a response to a specific piece of activity.

Rules and regulation of promotion

Promotion is regulated at three levels, and an answer should name all three. Law sets the hard floor: consumer protection, contract, privacy and data rules, and the specific limits on tobacco, alcohol, medicines and advertising to children. Regulation is the industry or government body that enforces the code. Self-regulation is the industry's own standards, which usually ask for truth, decency, no misleading price claims and clear identification of an ad.

The recurring offences are the same everywhere: a claim that cannot be proved, a price comparison against a figure that was never charged, a paid endorsement presented as an ordinary opinion, a competition whose rules are not disclosed, and data collected without consent. On social and mobile the platform's own policy is a fourth layer, and it can remove a campaign faster than any regulator.

Advertising from an Islamic perspective

The notes mark three dimensions: social, economic and ethical. The principles below are the ones a marketing course usually draws out, not a legal ruling.

DimensionThe principleWhat it rules out
EthicalTruthfulness and honesty in the claimExaggeration, deception, a promise the product cannot keep
EthicalDignity and modesty in the imageExploitation of the body, fear or shame to sell
SocialBenefit to society and no harmPromotion of products or habits that damage health, family or community
SocialFairness and good faith in the marketDiscrediting a competitor, exploiting the poor or the illiterate
EconomicFair exchange and no exploitation of needMonopoly pricing, hidden charges, interest-based inducement, waste
EconomicModeration in spendingExtravagant promotion that adds cost without adding value

The practical test is simple enough to write in an exam: would the customer, seeing the whole transaction and the whole message, still agree to it? If the answer depends on what was hidden, the promotion fails the ethical test whatever its sales result.
